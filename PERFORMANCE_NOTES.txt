Ring preview optimization — 2026-10-16

Implemented in order of expected benefit without reducing visual detail:

1. Reuse the current ring for appearance edits.
   Metal, finish, stone colors, engraving and existing inlay colors update only
   affected materials. Compatible materials keep their resource identities;
   gemstone color edits update optical uniforms and retain the shared studio.
   Geometry, camera scale, bounds and shadow maps stay intact. Dimension,
   setting, pattern and inlay-presence changes still rebuild the geometry.

2. Calculate each exact band-surface coordinate once per build.
   Smooth surfaces use indexed triangles to share vertex attributes. UV seam
   endpoints remain separate. Facets and tapered shoulders keep independent
   face normals. Triangle count, subdivision density, gemstone shaders,
   optical bounce count, antialiasing and preview resolution are unchanged.

SUBSEQUENT SHOULDER UPDATE — 2026-10-16
Cathedral shoulders now use one smooth arch, a continuous width taper and a
smooth thickness transition for flush side stones. The sweep evaluates the
arch directly, and matching seam vertices share their shading normals.
The Biegewinkel control adjusts the shoulder's angular extent around the ring
center: smaller gives a short, steep arch; larger gives a longer sweep.
Default 60 degrees; upper limit 100 degrees. The lower limit adapts to the
selected setting and side stones. The angle is saved/imported and exported.
Current verification: 58 unit tests, 648 raised cathedral configurations and
216 flush-seat configurations, plus the band and appearance checks above.

COMPLETE CATHEDRAL CONTROLS — 2026-10-16
Added independent shoulder thickness and arch curvature; side-stone spacing
and tilt; gallery, basket-base and split connections; and shoulder pave with
its own size, requested count and gemstone color. All choices round-trip in
saved JSON and appear in the text export. The UI reports the actual fitted
pave count when the requested row does not fit.

Flush seats retain enough metal beneath the stone; the setting height adapts
to preserve the finger opening and keep the seat floors clear of the band.
Split arms scale with the main stone. Camera framing fits the complete ring,
including tall fitted settings, in every preset and on viewport resize.

Final verification: 68 unit tests, the existing 648 raised and 216 flush-seat
mesh cases, plus 648 complete rings spanning three stone sizes, four settings,
three connection styles, three cuts, three shoulder thicknesses and pave on
or off. These contain 2,738 additional shoulder-pave seats. Assertions cover
finite geometry, finger clearance, main-stone clearance, separated gemstone
envelopes, open flush seats, split-arm counts and independent pave color edits
without geometry rebuilds. A camera regression projects the entire bounding
box in all three presets and a narrow viewport. Browser visual smoke testing
remains unavailable because browser security blocked file URLs.
